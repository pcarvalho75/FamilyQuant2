Signal recovery

Four readings, one reference total

Each reading below is a percentage of that run's recorded total ion current (TIC). They are different views of the signal and should not be added together.

A peptide feature is one peptide at one charge in one elution peak, counted once. Another charge or elution peak is a separate feature.

M+0 only

The first, monoisotopic peak of the identified and de novo peptide features, divided by the run's TIC. Heavier isotope peaks of those same features are left out of this reading.

Isotope envelopes (measured and identified)

The measured isotope peaks of those same features, including M+0, divided by the run's TIC. The bar label gives the recorded isotope limit; an isotope without accepted measured signal receives no credit.

Isotope envelopes (measured)

Isotope patterns found directly in the MS1 scans, with or without an accepted peptide sequence, divided by the run's TIC. This is an independent measurement with its own acceptance rules; it is not calibrated to a peptide false discovery rate (FDR).

TIC: the reference total

The ion current recorded for the run's MS1 scans, shown as 100%. It includes signal with and without assigned peptide features.

How to read the gaps

M+0 only against Isotope envelopes (measured and identified)

The increase comes from including the measured heavier isotope peaks of the same peptide features. It does not represent additional peptide identifications or newly found proteins.

PSM and de novo against Isotope envelopes (measured)

The detector can find isotope patterns that did not become accepted identified or de novo peptide features. The two methods make independent selections, so subtracting their totals does not measure a validated pool of unidentified peptides.

Isotope envelopes (measured) against TIC

This is signal outside the detector's accepted isotope patterns. The chart alone cannot classify the whole gap as noise, non-peptide material or biological signal, and the readings are not a biological partition of TIC.

The readings need not form perfectly nested sets. If their order is unexpected, inspect the stored run and method; the chart preserves the recorded values. A missing TIC or detector result stays unavailable, rather than becoming a zero measurement.

Runs, pooling and the de novo addition

Individual runs each use their own TIC. All runs pooled divides the summed signal by the summed TIC; it is not the mean of the run percentages, and these charts have no whiskers.

In Signal recovery components, the added de novo percentage points use pooled TIC. The relative increase uses the identified envelope as its baseline, so it answers a different question: how much signal de novo adds compared with identification alone. It is unavailable when that baseline is zero.

Normalize to changes the family charts. The recovery charts keep the fixed reference totals named beside their readings; select a bar or value for the exact number and its reference total.
